A Patient's Guide to Reading a Strain Profile

HOLYWEED Clinic Team · 6 min read

When practitioners discuss cannabis products, you'll often hear the same handful of terms — indica, sativa, hybrid, terpenes, THC percentage. Here's what they actually mean, and why they matter less than you might think.

Indica, sativa, and hybrid — a simplified map, not a rulebook

These categories originated as botanical classifications, and over time became shorthand for expected effects (indica for relaxation, sativa for energy). In practice, most cultivated strains today are hybrids, and individual response varies more by chemistry and by the person than by category alone.

Terpenes: the aroma compounds that shape the experience

Terpenes are the aromatic compounds responsible for a strain's smell — citrus, pine, earthy, floral. Beyond scent, researchers believe terpenes may influence how a strain feels in combination with THC and CBD, though this is still an active area of study. Your practitioner may ask about your preferences here as part of matching a product to you.

THC percentage isn't the whole story

A higher THC percentage doesn't automatically mean "better" or "stronger" in a useful sense — it mainly means a smaller amount can produce a more intense effect. For new patients, practitioners generally favor starting with lower THC concentrations and adjusting gradually rather than choosing the highest number on the shelf.

Why the same strain can affect two people differently

Body chemistry, tolerance, method of consumption, and even what you've eaten that day all influence how a given product feels. This is exactly why HOLYWEED's formulary is discussed in consultation rather than ordered independently — your practitioner factors in variables a strain name alone can't capture.
