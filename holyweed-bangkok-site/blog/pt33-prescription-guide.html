How the PT 33 Prescription Process Works in Thailand

HOLYWEED Clinic Team · 8 min read

If you're considering medical cannabis in Thailand, the process starts and ends with one document: the PT 33 prescription form. Since Thailand moved to a medical-only cannabis framework, every legal purchase of cannabis flower or extract requires a valid PT 33 issued by an authorized practitioner. Here's what that actually looks like in practice.

Who can issue a PT 33 prescription?

Under current Ministry of Public Health rules, only these practitioner types may prescribe medical cannabis:

  • Medical doctors
  • Thai traditional medicine doctors
  • Applied Thai traditional medicine doctors
  • Folk healers (registered practitioners)
  • Chinese medicine practitioners
  • Pharmacists
  • Dentists

At HOLYWEED, consultations are handled by our on-site medical doctor and Thai traditional medicine practitioner.

What happens during a consultation?

Your practitioner will review your medical history, current medications, and the symptoms or condition you're hoping to address. This isn't a formality — it's a genuine clinical assessment, and not every patient will be a suitable candidate for cannabis therapy.

What does the prescription cover?

If your practitioner determines cannabis is appropriate, they will issue a PT 33 specifying:

  • The strain or product type
  • Dosage guidance
  • Duration of use — up to a maximum of 30 days

Each PT 33 is valid for a single fill and cannot be refilled. If you need more after your supply runs out, you'll need a follow-up visit and a new prescription.

Filling your prescription

Prescriptions are filled in person at a licensed dispensary — we cannot fill or ship a prescription without you or your authorized representative present. Clinics are required to keep prescription records on file for at least one year, so bring valid ID to your appointment.

A few things that are no longer allowed

It's worth being clear about what's changed. Under current regulation:

  • Cannabis cannot be sold to walk-in customers without a prescription
  • Online sales and delivery of cannabis flower are not permitted
  • Recreational sale and advertising of cannabis products are prohibited
  • Public consumption remains illegal, regardless of prescription status

These rules exist to keep the medical framework intact, and clinics that don't follow them risk losing their license.
